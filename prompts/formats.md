## 1. Paragraphs only
Write 3 to 4 natural paragraphs, separated by one blank line. No headings, no bullets, no lists. Read like a real person describing their project.

## 2. Casual and raw
Write it like a busy client typing quickly: one or two loose paragraphs, conversational tone, short sentences, a little informal (for example "Hey," or "Looking for someone who..."). No headings, no bullets. Keep every fact but don't polish it too much.

## 3. Structured, no titles
Start with one short intro sentence. Then a blank line and 4 to 7 points, each on its own line starting with "- ". Then a blank line and one closing sentence. No section headings at all.

## 4. Headings with bullets
A 2 to 3 sentence intro paragraph. Then sections, each with a short heading on its own line ending with a colon (like "What you'll do:", "Requirements:", "Nice to have:"), and items under it on their own lines starting with "• ". One blank line between sections.

## 5. Numbered steps
A short intro paragraph. Then the work as a numbered list ("1.", "2.", "3." ...), one per line, each a full sentence. Then a short paragraph about who they are looking for. No headings.

## 6. Short and direct
Very concise: 3 to 5 short lines in total, each on its own line, with no bullets and no headings. Only the essentials: what is needed, key skills, and timing or budget if mentioned.

## 7. Story first
Open with the background (the business or situation and why they need help) in one paragraph. Then a paragraph on what exactly needs to be built or done. Then a paragraph on the ideal person. No headings, no bullets.

## 8. Q and A style
Write it as a few simple question lines each followed by the answer on the next line, for example "What is the project?", "What do we need?", "Who are we looking for?". Blank line between each pair. No bullets.

## 9. Paragraph plus skills line
Two paragraphs describing the project and the work. Then a blank line and a single line starting with "Skills needed: " followed by the main skills separated by commas. No other headings or bullets.

## 10. Mixed: intro, dashes, closing
One friendly intro paragraph. Then a line "Main tasks:" followed by 3 to 6 items on their own lines starting with "– ". Then a closing paragraph asking interested freelancers to share relevant past work. No other headings.
